# T06: Emergencies section, and Radiation copy and sources

Ticket 7 and the Radiation items of ticket 8 in `docs/tickets.md`.

## Why

Urgent guidance is scattered: the solar storm steps sit inside Radiation, and a reading that crosses an urgent
threshold leads nowhere. The Radiation page cites Phys.org and Space.com instead of primary sources.

## You own

- `src/scripts/emergencies.js` (already imported by `main.js`), `src/styles/emergencies.css`,
  `src/data/emergencies.js` (the content)
- `src/scripts/radiation.js`
- `test/emergencies.test.js`

## Do

### Emergencies

- New section registered with `window.AstroMedNav.register({ id: "emergencies", label: "Emergencies", before:
  "environment", render })`, so it is the first item in the sidebar.
- Organised by what is happening to the astronaut, not by body system. Entry screen: a short list of situations in
  plain words ("Chest pain or pressure", "Trouble breathing", "Sudden change in vision", "Severe headache or
  confusion", "Bleeding that will not stop", "Suspected fracture", "Flank pain (possible kidney stone)",
  "Thoughts of harming myself", "Solar storm warning", "Cabin air problem (CO2, pressure, smoke)"). Choosing one
  shows numbered steps to follow now, what to prepare, and when to wake or call the crew medical officer.
- Content lives in `src/data/emergencies.js`, one object per situation: `id`, `title`, `steps[]`, `source` (name
  and URL) or `designChoice: true`. Base the steps on a named, public source you can actually open and cite
  (for example NASA-STD-3001, NASA Human Research Program pages, standard first aid guidance from the Red Cross or
  the ERC/AHA basic life support guidelines). Never invent a dose, a drug or a threshold. Where you have no
  source, keep the steps to generic first aid, mark `designChoice: true`, and list the item under `SOURCE-NEEDED:`.
  No "call emergency services", no "see a doctor": the astronaut is on a mission; the escalation is the crew
  medical officer and, when contact exists, the flight surgeon.
- Move the solar storm steps from Radiation into the "Solar storm warning" situation. Radiation keeps a short
  summary and a button "Open solar storm steps" that calls `AstroMedNav.show("emergencies")` and opens that
  situation.
- Public API on `window.AstroMedEmergencies`: `open(situationId)` (shows the section on that situation) and
  `situations()` (ids and titles). Other sections will link here when a reading is urgent; you do not edit them.
- Works with no connection: everything is in the bundle, no fetch at run time.
- A line on the page: this is first-response guidance for a crew member without immediate ground support, not a
  diagnosis.

### Radiation copy and sources (ticket 8)

- Replace the Phys.org and Space.com citations with primary sources, and check that each figure in the page matches
  the source you cite: for dose rates in transit and on the Mars surface, the MSL/RAD papers (Zeitlin et al. 2013
  and Hassler et al. 2014, both in Science); for the lunar surface, the Chang'E-4 LND paper (Zhang et al. 2020,
  Science Advances) or NASA LRO/CRaTER pages; for the ISS, a NASA page. Open each source and confirm the number
  before citing it. If a figure in the page does not match its primary source, correct it and say so in your report.
- Rewrite the "Where you are going" instructions so they say plainly what the astronaut should enter and what the
  page does with it.
- The solar storm section follows the move described above.
